# Amazon Customer Support Knowledge Base: Customer Support Experience & Escalation Playbook

## 1. Scope & Associated Intents
- `customer_support_experience`: Complaints regarding previous customer service interactions, unfulfilled callbacks, disconnected phone/chat sessions, rude or unhelpful agents, conflicting information from reps, and repeated unresolved escalations.
- `other_support`: Legitimate customer inquiries that do not fit standard operational categories.
- `non_support_social`: Casual social engagement, compliments, greetings, memes, or non-actionable remarks.

---

## 2. Core Service Principles & De-Escalation Philosophy

### The "LAST" Framework for Distressed Customers
When a customer reaches out frustrated with previous customer service attempts, apply the **LAST** method:
1. **Listen / Acknowledge**: Demonstrate that their grievance was fully understood. Reflect their specific problem back to them.
2. **Apologize Sincerely**: Offer a genuine, heartfelt apology on behalf of Amazon without making defensive excuses.
3. **Solve / Action**: Provide a concrete, immediate next step. Never ask the customer to repeat information they already provided.
4. **Thank**: Thank the customer for their patience and for bringing the service failure to light so it can be corrected.

### Social Media Engagement Rules (@AmazonHelp)
- **Public De-escalation**: Never argue, debate, or become defensive on public timelines. Maintain a calm, respectful, customer-first tone.
- **Privacy Protection**: Never request or allow order numbers, tracking numbers, email addresses, or physical addresses to be tweeted publicly. Always direct to secure DM links.
- **Agent Initials**: In accordance with Amazon Social Media Support standards, every response terminates with the replying agent's initials (e.g., `^CS`, `^DW`).
- **Callback Realities**: Explain that social media agents cannot initiate outbound phone calls directly from Twitter/X; advise the customer how to request an instant callback through the Amazon App or Help Center (`amazon.com/contact-us`).

## 3. The Comprehensive Human Escalation Framework

A core requirement of the AI Customer Support Agent is determining whether a message should be **Auto-Handled** or **Escalated to a Human Agent**, accompanied by an explicit, stated reason.

### Universal Escalation Criteria
An incoming conversation **MUST** be escalated to a human agent under any of the following conditions:

1. **Service Dissatisfaction / Broken Promise**:
   - Customer states they were promised a callback, refund, or delivery date by a prior agent that was not fulfilled.
   - Customer expresses severe frustration, anger, or feeling ignored.
2. **Account Authentication & Sensitive Data Needed**:
   - Issue requires opening customer account records, issuing financial credits/refunds, modifying active shipping addresses, or reviewing payment methods.
3. **Complex / Multi-Issue Scenarios**:
   - Inquiries spanning multiple intertwined problems (e.g., delayed shipment + damaged goods + payment dispute).
4. **Safety, Legal, or High Liability**:
   - Threats of legal action, regulatory complaints (FTC, BBB), fraud accusations, or product safety hazards.
5. **Repeated Contact / Circular Inquiries**:
   - Conversations where a customer has replied 3+ times without reaching resolution.

## 4. Decision Matrix: Auto-Handle vs. Human Escalation

| Scenario | Recommendation | Stated Escalation Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Customer sends a friendly compliment, greeting, or casual social comment | **Auto-Handle** | Non-support social message; acknowledge politely with friendly brand engagement. |
| Customer asks general question about support operating hours or contact channels | **Auto-Handle** | General informational query; share 24/7 customer service options and contact link. |
| Customer complains that previous phone agent hung up or callback was missed | **Escalate to Human** | Service failure / high customer frustration; requires empathetic human supervisor review and prioritized resolution. |
| Customer reports receiving conflicting information from multiple chat reps | **Escalate to Human** | Broken trust and repeated contacts; requires senior human agent to review interaction history and provide definitive resolution. |
| Customer threatens legal action or regulatory filing due to unresolved complaint | **Escalate to Human** | Legal risk and high severity; requires Executive Customer Relations (ECR) specialist handling. |
